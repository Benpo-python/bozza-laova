The heritage of a place is more than what can be photographed.

It lives in the gestures passed down, the stories told, the natural resources used with care and the ways communities organise their lives.

Who we are

Plateforme LAOVA (Héritage) was born of the wish to help safeguard this living heritage while supporting its transmission to new generations. A Malagasy non-profit association founded in Maroantsetra in 2022, LAOVA brings together research and memory, nature conservation, learning, community initiatives and cooperation.

We believe that safeguarding and promotion go hand in hand: what deserves protection must also be known, recognised and passed on, in ways that respect people and ecosystems.

Working as a platform allows us to bring complementary knowledge and skills to each mission. In this way we seek to make heritage protection a space for dialogue, participation and concrete action, in the service of the communities concerned.

Governance

A Malagasy non-profit association founded on 2 July 2022 in Maroantsetra, governed by its General Assembly and its Executive Board.

Vision

A Madagascar where people, aware of their cultural and natural heritage, have the means to safeguard it, pass it on and keep it alive.

This vision recognises the value of traditional knowledge without setting tradition against innovation. It favours change led by communities, rather than conservation staged from the outside.

MissionFive verbs

What we do, in practice.

LAOVA’s statutory purpose: to safeguard, promote and transmit the cultural and natural heritage of Madagascar, by strengthening civic engagement and the connection between tradition and modernity.

  • Safeguardheritage, ecosystems, memories and endangered know-how.
  • Understandand document local realities through research, testimony and digital tools.
  • Transmitknowledge and practices through education, learning and dialogue between generations.
  • Promotecultural and natural wealth responsibly, creating opportunities for those who hold it.
  • Connectand act, bringing complementary skills, communities and partners together around concrete projects.

Values

Six principles that guide every project.

Respect for cultures and people

Communities take part in defining the initiatives that concern them; their voices are not incidental.

Solidarity between generations

Young people are not only recipients of transmission: they become actors in its renewal.

Civic engagement

Heritage is a shared responsibility; LAOVA encourages forms of participation suited to the territory.

Dialogue and intercultural openness

Encounters should foster mutual understanding, without exoticising or appropriating practices.

Ethical and inclusive innovation

Digital tools, research and new economic models are useful when they strengthen local capacity.

Balance between people and nature

Productive practices take account of resources, ecosystems and their renewal.

Why “platform”

An organisation shaped by each mission.

The word “platform” describes a way of organising skills and cooperation, not just a brand name.

LAOVA has an associative governance and shared coordination. Depending on the project, it forms or mobilises multidisciplinary teams of members, professionals, local actors and partners.

A heritage documentation mission will call on community resource persons, historians, anthropologists and audiovisual technicians; an ecological restoration project will need botanical, mapping, environmental monitoring and community facilitation skills; an educational activity will bring together educators, farmers, facilitators and craftspeople.

Cross-cutting functions — coordination, administration, financial management, technical follow-up, monitoring and evaluation, and partner dialogue — ensure the continuity of the association and keep projects from becoming separate entities without a shared identity.

The six mission areas

Territory

Maroantsetra, at the head of Antongil Bay.

In north-east Madagascar, between rainforest, wetlands and coast, this territory shows in concrete terms how biodiversity, plant resources, housing, crafts, farming and oral heritage are connected.

Our statutes cover the cultural and natural heritage of Madagascar; our interventions are precisely located, and we do not suggest a presence in every region.

Internationally, LAOVA develops collaborations with universities, associations, foundations, researchers and networks — presented as cooperation between responsible partners, never as outside supervision of communities.

Reference

Frame of thoughtOur approach is aligned with the principles of the 2003 UNESCO Convention for the Safeguarding of the Intangible Cultural Heritage, which includes identification, documentation, promotion and transmission within safeguarding. This reference informs our approach; it does not imply any institutional affiliation.
