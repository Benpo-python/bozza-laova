Six ways of serving one mission.

LAOVA is neither solely a conservation organisation, nor solely a cultural body, nor solely a farming initiative. These areas describe fields of action and the skills they draw on; they are not permanent administrative departments.

AMission area

Heritage, memory and research

Identifying, documenting and helping to transmit tangible and intangible heritage.

Oral memory, cultural practices, traditional architecture and crafts, local history and place-based knowledge. Photographic, audiovisual and digital tools support documentation carried out with the consent of knowledge holders.

Skills involved

Researchers, community resource persons, historians, anthropologists, archivists, photographers and translators.

BMission area

Nature, biodiversity and living resources

Preserving resources, preserving knowledge.

In Antongil Bay, natural resources sustain biodiversity, the local economy and the continuity of cultural practices. We seek to understand the relationships between ecosystems, practices and livelihoods, to support the protection and restoration of resources and to encourage their sustainable management.

Protecting nature also means preserving the conditions in which heritage can continue to live.

Skills involved

Environmental technicians, ecologists, GIS specialists, monitoring officers and community liaisons.

Penja, Raffia and Ravinala project

CMission area

Education and knowledge transmission

Learning from the territory, passing on for tomorrow.

Knowledge stays alive when it can be shared. We want to create learning opportunities rooted in local knowledge: intergenerational encounters, activities with young people, field-based learning, environmental awareness, farming and craft practices.

The educational purpose of the community farm illustrates this ambition: turning rural practices and the cycles of living things into tools for understanding, responsibility and transmission.

Skills involved

Educators, partner teachers, facilitators, craftspeople, farmers, livestock keepers and trainers.

The community farm

DMission area

Community development and responsible economy

Strengthening capacity, income and autonomy without depleting resources.

We support initiatives in craft value chains, agro-pastoral activities, training and local economic organisation. Each approach is adapted to expressed needs and real capacities; none is imposed from outside.

Skills involved

Local development officers, rural technicians, project managers, trade experts and community representatives.

EMission area

Digital documentation, creation and communication

Producing archives and stories, and sharing them with respect.

Archives, stories, educational materials, photographs and video bring initiatives and knowledge to light. Part of this work may become an open resource when rights holders and knowledge holders agree: digital safeguarding alone never authorises public release.

Skills involved

Audiovisual technicians, content creators, digital archivists, communicators and documentalists.

FMission area

Intercultural encounter and Hasina Immersion

Facilitating experiences of discovery, dialogue and, where relevant, participation.

This activity is community and cultural mediation, not the commercial sale of tourist stays. The people met can accept, decline or adapt any activity.

Skills involved

Local guides, cultural mediators, logistics coordinators and community resource persons, depending on the activities agreed.

Cross-cutting

Coordination and partnerships

Every project relies on the same standards: coordination, administration, financial management, technical follow-up, monitoring and evaluation, and partner dialogue. This is what makes the six areas one organisation.
